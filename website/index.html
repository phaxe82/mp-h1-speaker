Build & tuning notes

Polarity

Wire the compression driver inverted as a starting point — normal for a 2nd‑order electrical high-pass at this driver spacing. Sweep both polarities on-axis at 1 m and keep whichever gives a smooth sum (not a suckout) through 1–1.5 kHz in REW.

Level trim

If the horn reads hot or quiet against the woofer, swap R1 between 5.6–6.8 Ω and R2 between 2.2–3.3 Ω for roughly ±1.5 dB of adjustment without touching C1 or L1.

Upstream DSP

Both drivers share one amp channel, so there's one DSP EQ curve per box, not one per driver — not shown here since it's active, not passive. A 40 Hz high-pass (24 dB/oct) and a low-shelf below 100 Hz sit in that shared curve. That's safe to apply globally: below 1 kHz only the woofer is radiating anyway, so those filters have nothing to act on in the compression driver's output. The same logic applies to any top-octave shelf — above ~5 kHz only the horn is playing.

Board assembly

Board

A ~200 × 130 mm offcut of 6 mm ply or MDF, one per speaker. Drill hole pairs beside each part for cable ties, plus four corner holes to screw it to the cabinet floor — clear of the port airflow and not directly behind the woofer magnet.

Resistors

Raise R1 and R2 ~6–8 mm off the board on their own legs and keep them away from C1. They're the only parts that get hot; the ceramic body shouldn't touch wood and the cap shouldn't cook. Give them air.

Cap & coil

Lie both flat, two cable ties each through the drilled holes (or neutral-cure silicone / hot glue on the cap — never on the resistors). Keep the air coil ≥50 mm from any steel bracket or the woofer magnet, and don't line its axis up with the woofer.

Wiring

Point-to-point using the component legs as the main conductors — they're already ~1 mm. Add short 18 AWG hookup wire for the runs to the terminal cup and drivers; twist junctions tight before soldering and sleeve the ground-bus joints.

Build first

Solder the board, clip it to the amp on the bench, then sweep in REW to set driver polarity and confirm the crossover before mounting in the box. A solder joint is far easier to fix on the bench than through a driver cut-out.
